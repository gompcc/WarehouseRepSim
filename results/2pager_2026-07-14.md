# AGV Warehouse Simulation — Status, Learnings & Operational Implications
**2026-07-14 · for Gompels HealthCare AGV rollout · sim repo: WarehouseRepSim**

Business context: current ops ≈1,500 picks/hr; target 2,000 with headroom to
3,000. Real trials cost ~£1,000 each — the sim's job is to decide *what is
worth trialling*. Throughput (orders/hr, picks/hr) is the decision metric.
Glossary: a **line** = one SKU on an order (line == pick); an order = 1+ lines.

---

## 1. Where we've got to

The sim is a full closed loop of the proposed operation: carts spawn at the
Box Depot (60 s load), AGVs ferry them across 9 pick stations, **human
pickers** walk real distances to a **2,000-SKU catalog** (one slot per SKU,
bi-level racking, calibrated ≈31 s/pick on the baseline layout), and a cart
leaves a station only when its lines there are picked. Model realism as of
today: orders are **mean 20 lines, SD 9** (January trials: 16–20 lines/cart),
sampled by SKU popularity (hot SKU ≈ 90× the coldest); **pickers cannot cross
the highway**, so each station serves only its own side (west: S1/S3 ·
central: S2/S4/S6/S8 · east: S5/S7/S9).

Everything decision-relevant is a toggle under a controlled experiment
harness (paired seeds, frozen walk calibration, demand identical across arms
— checksum-verified): 4 slotting strategies (sequential, aisle-proximal,
fibonacci, velocity), dispatch strategies (predicted-fill station choice,
Hungarian assignment), and fleet size — GUI toggles now auto-retarget the
fleet to each combo's optimum. The GUI (fits a 13" MBP) shows station zones
color-matched to their aisles, live picker stats, and an orders/hr strip.

## 2. What we've learned

**L1 — With the highway constraint, slotting is a ~3× lever, not a 3% one.**
Fresh 8h runs (table below): sequential slotting delivers **11.7** steady
orders/hr; velocity (ABC) slotting **32.3** (+176%), picks/hr 262 → 626.
Two mechanisms compound:
*Walk*: velocity cuts demand-weighted walk 18.1 → 13.6 m one-way (−25%).
*Balance* (dominant): sequential piles the popular SKUs into one side's
zones — S3's picker runs 99% busy with its station 100% full while S5's
picker is 2% busy; the whole loop queues behind one station. Velocity
spreads hot SKUs near every station (busy range 42–95%) and the loop flows.
Figures: `results/figures/walk_time_by_slotting.png`, `slotting_popularity_maps.png`.

**L2 — Fibonacci rings are worse than doing nothing.** 18.8 m demand-weighted
walk vs 18.1 sequential: rings around the AGV track optimize distance to the
*track*, but pickers launch from their *stations*. Wrong origin, negative
result — cheap to learn in sim, expensive on a real floor.

**L3 — The binding constraint is station dwell, and it moves with demand.**
Under the old flat order model the floor was picker-bound (81% busy). Under
realistic orders neither pickers nor AGVs saturate in aggregate — the
constraint is *specific stations*: carts holding scarce station slots while
their lines are picked. Fixing dwell (walk time + zone balance) tripled
throughput with zero hardware. Aggregate utilization numbers hide this;
per-station stats expose it.

**L4 — Dispatch intelligence is second-order until the layout is right.**
Predicted-fill + Hungarian dispatch: ±0% on the imbalanced sequential layout
(the bottleneck station is full no matter how cleverly you route), ~+1% on
velocity. Under the older, better-balanced model it was worth +2–5%
consistently across three fleet regimes. Sequence matters: layout first,
dispatch tuning after.

**L5 — More stations per order = more transport legs = a standing tax.**
20-line orders touch ~6.5 stations. Station-visit count, not line count,
drives AGV work and cycle time. Batching an order's lines into fewer zones
at release attacks this for free in software.

**L6 — Methodology matters as much as mechanisms.** Traps already caught:
orders sampled per-zone gave each slotting arm different demand (fixed:
SKU-space sampling, checksum-verified); re-tuning a policy constant
mid-experiment flipped its sign (fixed: frozen calibration rule); results
predating today's model changes are not comparable and have been retired.

## 3. Operational implications for Gompels

1. **Re-slot by velocity before anything else.** In-sim it is the difference
   between 11.7 and 32.3 orders/hr under the realistic model. Software +
   shelf-moves, no capex. Also the path toward 240 lines/picker/hr:
   15 s/pick needs walk ≈ 5 s — velocity slotting plus tighter zones.
2. **Balance picker workload across zones, not just distance.** Because
   pickers can't cross the highway, whatever lives on a picker's side IS
   their workload. Slot placement must equalize *demand per zone* — one hot
   zone throttles the entire circuit (S3 at 99% while S5 idles at 2%).
3. **Don't size the fleet before fixing dwell.** Neither pickers nor AGVs
   were saturated in aggregate while throughput was 11.7 — buying more of
   either would have bought nothing. "Loading up the circuit with carts"
   isn't the answer; past the congestion cliff more carts *reduce*
   throughput. Each dispatch policy also has its own optimal fleet
   (per-combo optimization re-running under the current model).
4. **Batch an order's lines into as few zones as possible** at release —
   the stations-per-order tax (L5) is set upstream by the WMS, free to fix.
5. **Dispatch by predicted station fill, not current fill — but after
   layout.** Real (+2–5% when the floor is balanced), irrelevant when one
   station is the bottleneck.
6. **Trial shortlist** (expected value order): velocity re-slot +
   demand-balanced zones → zone-batched order release → predicted-fill
   dispatch.
