# Findings — Slotting, Picker & Dispatch Study
**2026-07-14 · 40 valid runs · 8h each · 10 AGVs / 25 carts · canonical order
book (15h @ 3,000 lines/hr, sizes N(20,9)) · 5 paired seeds (11/42/77/101/137)
· side-constrained zoning (pickers never cross the highway) · pick cycle
calibrated μ30s σ10s · full tables: `results/experiment_matrix.md`, raw:
`results/runs/matrix_rows.json`.**

Decision rule (pre-registered): adopt an arm iff paired mean ≥ **+3%** vs its
baseline and positive on ≥4/5 seeds. Validity gates (0 teleports, 0 carts
released early) passed on all 40 runs.

---

## A. Product layout (slotting)

| Arm | Steady orders/hr (seed min–max) | Lines/picker/hr | Live pick cycle | Busy CV |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| sequential | 16.7 (15.5–17.2) | 37.6 | 28.1 s | 0.98 |
| aisle_proximal | 16.8 (15.7–17.6) | 37.8 | 27.9 s | 0.98 |
| **fibonacci** | **24.9 (23.6–25.5)** | **54.5** | 29.5 s | **0.78** |

**Fibonacci wins by +49% with seed ranges fully disjoint** — adopt.
(Figures: `matrix_slotting.png`, `slotting_popularity_maps.png`.)

**The mechanism is workload balance, not walk distance.** Fibonacci actually
has the *longest* pick cycle of the three (29.5 s vs 28.1) and the worst
demand-weighted walk (18.8 m vs 18.1). It wins because spreading hot SKUs
in rings around the loop equalizes demand across all nine stations' zones
(picker-busy CV 0.78 vs 0.98). Sequential and aisle_proximal pile the
popular SKUs into one or two zones; with pickers unable to cross the
highway, the hottest station's queue throttles the entire circuit while
seven pickers idle. **Walking-distance optimization is the wrong objective
under zoned, side-constrained picking; demand-per-zone balance is the right
one.** (aisle_proximal ≈ sequential: rearranging *within* aisles doesn't
change what each zone owns.)

## A2. Picker strategy (static vs dynamic-within-side)

| Slotting | Static | Dynamic | Δ |
|---|---|---|---|
| sequential | 16.7 | 21.3 (19.3–22.4) | **+27%** |
| fibonacci | 24.9 | **32.4 (31.6–33.5)** | **+30%** |

**Dynamic pickers clear the bar on both layouts — adopt.** Idle pickers
walking to their side's worst backlog (~1,000–1,300 relocations per 8h, the
walking time all charged as busy) compresses busy CV further (0.78 → 0.69)
and cuts order cycle time 56 → 45 min. Notably dynamic does NOT substitute
for good slotting: it recovers only part of sequential's imbalance (21.3
vs fibonacci-static's 24.9) because roaming is confined to each side of the
highway. The two levers compound rather than overlap.
(Figure: `matrix_pickers.png`.)

## B. Dispatch strategies (on fibonacci/static)

| Dispatch | Steady orders/hr (min–max) |
|---|---|
| baseline | 24.9 (23.6–25.5) |
| ETA reservations | 25.3 (24.0–26.0) |
| Hungarian | 21.9 (**9.9**–26.1) |
| ETA + Hungarian | 25.3 (24.0–26.5) |

**No dispatch strategy clears the +3% bar — do not adopt any.** ETA is
+1.6% with overlapping ranges. **Hungarian alone is actively dangerous**:
one seed collapsed to 9.9 orders/hr (a matching-induced starvation regime),
so even its mean hides tail risk; ETA appears to mask it when combined.
Dispatch cleverness cannot move a floor whose constraint is station-zone
workload. (Figure: `matrix_dispatch.png`.)

## Recommended configuration & the remaining gap

**Fibonacci slotting + dynamic pickers + baseline dispatch: 32.4 steady
orders/hr ≈ 636 picks/hr, 70.7 lines/picker/hr** — 1.94× the
sequential/static baseline with zero additional hardware, at 66% picker
utilization and 80% AGV utilization.

Against the business target (2,000–3,000 picks/hr): the sim floor at this
scale (9 pickers, 10 AGVs, 25 carts) delivers ~636. Closing the rest is
capacity and cycle time, not policy: 70.7 lines/picker/hr against a ~120
ceiling at the 30 s pick cycle says ~40% of remaining headroom is idle/
imbalance (staffing hot stations — now testable via click-to-hire — plus
more AGVs, since the winner runs 80% AGV-utilized), and beyond ~120 only a
shorter pick cycle (tighter zones, batching lines per visit) reaches the
240 lines/picker/hr target.

## Method notes (for reproduction)

Identical demand across arms by construction (orders drawn from the
committed book; seeds are window offsets — paired per seed). Calibration
frozen (never refit per arm). Warm-up excluded (first 30 min). Old results
(pre-2026-07-14 model: velocity slotting, 45 s depot, 10 s grab,
unconstrained zoning) are not comparable and were retired. Known noise:
the stuck-watchdog flags carts legitimately waiting (queued for pickers or
for transport); teleports/left-early are the physics gates. Reproduce:
`./venv/bin/python experiments/run_matrix.py`.
